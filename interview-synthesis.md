## Section 1: Who Is Lee

### Core Identity: Artist First

Lee's core identity is **"I am an artist"** - this is how he answers when people ask what he does. This isn't a hobby or side interest; it's the foundational principle of how he structures his life.

**The Upside-Down Maslow Pyramid**: Lee lives by an inverted hierarchy of needs where art and self-actualization form the *foundation*, with survival needs at the top as a *consequence*. As a business owner with no plan, an engineer with no degree and no job, he has "none of those things, but I do have lots of big plans."

**Harvey Mudd Dropout**: Attended Harvey Mudd College pursuing math/CS degree. During an engineering midterm he'd skipped class for, stared at problems with no idea how to solve them. *Office Space* was popular at the time - the cubicle life looked like death. He turned in a blank exam (guaranteed F) and dropped out for the second and final time. Better to "give it a shot being an artist while young and able to tolerate poverty, figuring could always come back to computers down the line."

### Pacific Puzzleworks: The Art Itself

**Started Early**: Designing puzzles since age 11-12. Used campus woodshop to blow off steam making designs from the early internet. Found complete how-to guide online for hobby art puzzle business.

**Real Art Definition**: "Real ART is something that evokes an emotion in the audience. Better artists wield more control over the emotional journey they orchestrate." Lee's puzzles create a specific emotional journey: fancy/want to touch → nice → confused/frustrated → over my head/hate it → AHA → wait no → YES → love it → how much → nevermind. "Playing people like a magician showing off the work."

**The Realization**: The art is tied to how he presents it as the artist. The things created are byproducts of the artist lifestyle, not the art itself.

**Business Reality**: "Shitty at business side - always running out of money because chasing passionate ideas instead of nonexistent business plan."

### Precision and Craftsmanship

**Machinist-Level Tolerances**: Started woodworking as young kid. Got into metal machining to build custom table saw fixtures. Learned measurement/setup techniques from machine shops, applied them to woodworking to achieve **0.001 inch or less** - actual machinist tolerances on a standard table saw using custom jigs.

**The Architect's Lesson**: At age 15, worked for an architect building models of his designs for clients. The architect said: "Perfection is mandatory. 99% is not good enough." Frustrating at 15, but it stuck.

**Tactile Experience**: "Fingers already know how precision feels from watches, knives, guns, cabinetry, jewelry boxes, precious baubles. When something moves so smoothly you can feel the air inside acting like pillow, or little sounds as parts slide with perfect fit - massively nice tactile experience. Can measure it without micrometers."

**Evolution to "Good Enough"**: Now focuses more on "good enough for what it's designed to accomplish" rather than "perfection is mandatory" mindset. But the dual requirements remain: KNOWING how to do finest quality work AND CARING enough to WANT to do it meticulously.

**Precision's Dark Side**: Puzzle art became too precious and expensive. Reluctant to let anyone handle nice stuff because damage = massive value loss. **HATES how precious the items are**. This is the dark side of chasing perfection: pigeonholed into category of non-fun because folks too terrified to play with toys they bought. Perfection killed playability.

### Optimization Mindset

**Single-Chip Thinking**: Core philosophy across domains - sailing trim, CNC machining, agent workflows. "Think about ONE SINGLE CHIP from one edge of cutter. Optimize that one chip across whole operation in aggregate like calculus equation."

**Concrete CNC Example**: HSM (high speed machining) - full depth of cut, minimal radial stepovers, very fast feedrates. "Cut stepover in half + double feedrate = same runtime but cutter lasts 5-10x longer. Chips easier to handle. Then double depth of cut = twice as fast AND still lasts 5-10x longer."

**Agentic Programming Parallel**: "Feels same way about agentic programming right now. Building Henhouse to get that same kind of massive productivity boost like CNC toolpath programming by being smart about planning to nth degree of fine grained resolution."

### Heinlein Generalist

**"Specialization is for insects"** - This Heinlein quote "resonates strongly through every pore of being."

**Cross-Pollination Advantage**: "Huge advantage for ability to take knowledge from totally disparate realms and cross them together to do things in new ways that nobody has thought of before, or at least bothered trying."

**Financial Cost**: "Sometimes worries from business owner standpoint about spreading thin instead of focusing on one product that sells well. Puzzles are terrible way to make money - everyone in niche understands this well."

**Why Persist**: "Stubborn about wanting to push forward the vision because of passion and sense of inner purpose or drive toward some muse being chased."

**Flow State as Exercise**: Breadth is simply exercise to stay in flow state. Doesn't matter what learning new as long as it's something - certain will find way to apply it to all other things too.

### Music and Manifestation

**Piano as Visualization Tool**: Sought piano specifically to solve visualization problem with guitar/music theory. Wanted "typewriter simple" instrument where entire range of octaves laid out in black and white. Needed to SEE the patterns: intervals, chords, circle of fifths. Guitar required juggling technical playing challenge AND theory comprehension simultaneously.

**What Clicked**: "Seeing patterns in mind's eye - the whole big picture of how notes and chords relate to each other and WHY. Understanding = capability and confidence to play around creating freely, not attempting specific thing but letting music flow through him like a conduit."

**Music's Freedom**: Music gives what puzzles don't: can share for free for fun, costs nothing besides time (which he enjoys anyway). Nothing lost if it lands on deaf ears because he still likes it. Complete freedom from economic pressure. (3D printing plastic puzzles offers similar redemption - practically free to give away, nobody scared to touch because lacks preciousness.)

## Section 2: Why Henhouse Exists

### The Real Problems Being Solved

#### Problem 1: The 250k Puzzle Collection

**Scale**: World Puzzle Foundation castle in Italy houses world's largest collection (250k+ puzzles).

**Relationship**: George has been colleague/friend since Lee was 21 (paid $1000 for week apprenticeship with "whiz kid"). Can visit anytime, stay as long as he likes, full access to document as he sees fit.

**Reality Check**: This is a REAL institution open to public, not theory. Connected to IPP - this summer they host IPP 2026.

**The Task**: Take pictures/video, vacuum up data, feed to curator agents. Plan: automated pipeline with agent assistance to ingest A LOT quickly. Data per puzzle: pictures, text descriptions, scans, size, weight, materials, designer, craftsman, edition size, relative difficulty, solution info - the more the better.

**Connection to 500k Vision**: Seed it well with sweet features, other folks will start adding content and it fills itself the rest of the way in. Doesn't need to manually scan all 500k - just needs infrastructure to hold it.

#### Problem 2: The All Known Puzzles Database (500k Vision)

**What It Is**: Master resource people want to exist. Like a plant database for puzzles - folks often don't know what they have or are looking to acquire.

**Use Cases**:
- **Identification**: Inheritance scenarios, specialty auctioneers make money because they CAN identify
- **Artisans**: Marketing and info distribution
- **Collectors**: Track real-time values like stock ticker, see demand from potential buyers, make wishlists
- **Buyers**: Assemble shopping carts of puzzles to track down
- **Marketplace**: System connects buyers with sellers for trades/sales

**Why Nobody Built It**: Scope and size. TONS of fine-grained info snippets. Many deep relational ties - not simple spreadsheet. Breadth of what counts as puzzle. Lack of central location to see examples.

**Recent Developments - Allan Slocum Call**: Couple months ago sent email to puzzle list about pausing puzzles for AI tools. Led to long call with Allan Slocum (Jerry Slocum's son - Jerry founded IPP, Allan runs nonprofit). Jerry donated puzzles to U of Indiana - everything out of sight, not documented well. Allan asked specifically: would Henhouse work for All Known Puzzles database? Allan thinks huge benefit to community, suggested his org could help fund (no dollar amount, not sole contributor).

**Community Context**: Small community (~1-2k serious collectors worldwide). Particularly brainy and tech savvy crowd. Appreciation for fine details of analog stuff. Precedent: Discord community, collaborative craftsmen projects. IPP registration site Lee built in 2009 still in use 15 years later - that project is what got him fired up to design first Page base class CMS system!

**Guild Vision**: Present as "international mechanical puzzle makers guild" - provide database as service. Moneymaker: extract small percentages when items sell on secondary market. Designers/makers get percentage when their stuff sells. Extract small percent to cover server fees. More like Craigslist than Etsy/Shopify - runs on "some guy's server in a closet," not giant corporation. The "investors" are fellow craftsmen who put their info in system.

**Spoiler Problem**: Puzzle folks do NOT want ah-ha moment spoiled. Discord has spoiler tags. Legacy system has cookie toggle for spoilers shown/hidden - curates which images appear so you don't stumble onto something you wish you could unsee.

**Nature**: Combo: commercial venture + community service + personal satisfaction (Lee is part of community).

#### Problem 3: Business Accounting Chaos (20k Receipts)

**Artist Lifestyle Reality**: 10 years without filing taxes. City of Oakland got irate about form he forgot - wanted to see business taxes. Had never filed any. At least kept piles of receipts and bank records "just in case."

**Forensic Accounting**: Needed to systematically do forensic accounting for entire decade. 20k scanned receipts (JPEGs). Very un-fun period.

**QuickBooks Dealbreaker**: Really wanted to try QuickBooks but totally dissatisfied. DEALBREAKER: no ability to attach actual scans to records. Best he could find: custom field to write down filename. "That seemed like terrible UX vs having scans be first-class citizens." As far as he could tell, no other system did what he wanted, so he built it. (That was 15 years ago, maybe things have changed.)

**Custom Tailoring**: System tailored exactly to his business needs for tax filing. Used TurboTax in past - AWFUL, takes SO MUCH EFFORT. Now knows how tax forms work, can slam out filing super fast pen and paper. System calculates values, knows where each number belongs. Doesn't need wizard interface, just needs final results.

**Audit Confidence**: Needs visibility into every step of methodology that generated numbers so confident can handle any audit. Has every scanned document to prove truthfulness of calculations.

**Current State**: Currently 5 years behind again! Plan: upload new images in batches, assign accountant agents to read OCR results, agents organize scans into appropriate page types, agents set up transfers between accounts/sales/statements. Agents do lions share of initial prep work so he can focus on fine tuning rather than bulk data entry.

#### Problem 4: Deep Hierarchy (Family-Design-Run-Instance)

**Not Product-Variant-Run-Unit**: It's **Family-Design-Production Run-Instance**.
- **Family**: "rectilinear 6 piece burrs"
- **Design**: "L5-notchable" (Bill Cutler)
- **Production Run**: Lee's batch of 10 in various woods
- **Instance**: Each specific wood combo with photos

**Curation Permissions at Each Level**:
- Designer (Bill Cutler) curates the L5-notchable page
- Lee curates his batch page
- 10 customers can curate their respective instance pages

**Why It Matters**: L5-notchable is just ONE cherry-picked example from MILLIONS or BILLIONS of possible rectilinear 6 piece burr puzzles. Hundreds of folks have made L5-notchable, not just him. How can we assign curation permissions at proper fine grain level? Nobody would ever fully document billions, but for any cherry-picked enough that someone bothered making a copy, there ought to be a place to file it in broader hierarchy.

**What Breaks if Flattened**: "Flattening it all would turn into big question of what level of hierarchy you decide to flatten - just chasing ones own tail. Only answer that makes sense: don't flatten anything, have it all hierarchical and relational instead."

**Categorization Problem**: Need to categorize gets to be pigeonhole. Burrs, boxes, but what about box that disassembles like burr? Solution: doesn't matter where top-level goes, so long as inside of that it follows proper hierarchy. Then have relational fields to assign attributes like "is box" or "is burr."

**Cross-References**: Who designed, manufactured, took picture, is registered owner - in all cases could be multiple values for collaborative efforts. Any item person contributed to design should show up in that person's catalog under their tilde page.

**Spreadsheet Limitations**: Fine for collection of 100 puzzles. Stretch for artisan's body of work. Would NEVER suffice for All Known Puzzles concept with room for ANYONE to contribute.

#### Problem 5: CNC Tool Management (Real SAAS Revenue!)

**Real Use Case**: Worked in several CNC machine shops. Already implemented tool tracker system for one shop he used to work at. Actively hosts it for them on same box henhouse.ai runs on as SAAS. That machine shop uses it probably every day. **This is the only one of his systems that actually currently DOES bring in any money as SAAS.**

**Pain Point**: Cutters often subtly different in ways easy for operator-level machinist to mistake. Night shift scenario where normal person (foreman, tool crib attendant) not there and operator needs tool replacement. They need to navigate into someone else's realms of expertise.

**Solution**: All documented well, easy to navigate and search, clearly pointing to physical location (cabinet 3, top shelf, drawer 87). Helps folks keep eye on inventory to ensure not running out unexpectedly, know consumption rate, audit inventory to detect disappearing cutters.

**Current State**: Code is super legacy - nothing to do with Page system, just hard-coded minimal CRUD. Suspects it would be trivial task for agent to refactor into proper Henhouse. Example improvement: being able to upload photos of what cutter looks like would be HUGE.

## Section 3: What Makes Henhouse Different

### Differentiator 1: Debug System (On-Demand, User-Facing)

**The 3 F's**: Show very clearly which **Folder, File, and Function** each message comes from so execution flow is dead-simple obvious.

**On-Demand, Not File Logging**: Debug output appears in your terminal/response when you activate it with flags, not in log files on server. Three tiers:
1. `trace_in/trace_out` (call stack push/pop)
2. `log` (general purpose state conveying)
3. `debug` (separate channel for tracking specific behavior)

All no-opped lambdas unless demanded.

**Killer Use Case**: "Can fire up new chat session, simply paste results of 'hen some-action -log', agent needs ZERO further explanation of: (1) what kind of system is running, (2) what business logic is happening, (3) flow of execution, (4) how codebase is broken into modules, (5) exactly where to look for more details about any part of process. That is why it has been so helpful."

**Agent Fights**: Agents trained on production sites with polished aesthetics. They didn't understand why debug info wouldn't just go into logfiles on server. He wanted super powerful tool for himself to use at every step.

**Forward-Facing Examples**: Any URL can add `?log=1` to show full shebang just like command line. Had to force agents to pass all debug info through MCP calls - packing up (dehydrating) to be legit JSON-RPC 2.0 format, then rehydrating in mcp-wrapper script.

**Self-Debug via MCP**: Add arg `debug=1` to any MCP call and see it in response. Example: ask what user tier you're running as, debug response shows that PLUS what file to point to for logic of tier detection.

**Learned from HN**: Learned kwargs pattern from Hacker News (improvement opportunity: string concatenation should happen inside demanded method). Also learned about document databases from HN - led to another big refactor.

### Differentiator 2: Self-Hosting / Meta Philosophy

**What Self-Doc Means**: Each file has corresponding source-code-file page where page-text is in-depth explanation of what file is and how it works. Separates documentation from implementation. Planning to tie into help system so asking for help can reference "man pages."

**What Comes Included**: Source-code-file pages, plus any other generic pages/sub-pages created to explain in blog format how any aspect works. Design philosophy discussions. About page (tilde-leekrasnow page) where creator can muse about motivations.

**Markdown vs Self-Doc**: Also shipping docs in markdown files, but those are "context engineered dumps" for agents to consume, not interface humans will enjoy. Looks at markdown as mostly "for agents" thing (happens to be format humans can parse well too).

**Artistic Statement**: "Like planting seed and letting it grow and resow on its own, rather than running factory outputting something which must remain in operation else existence of 'somethings' stops. Properly formatted seed = git repository with everything inside so henhouse.ai doesn't actually need to exist."

**Core Value Proposition**: NOT because they need some CMS system but rather because it's good way to handle context engineering when dealing with swarms of agents working on something together. Those agents never need to touch henhouse code at all - just use MCP server.

**Scratchpad Importance**: "Stellar workflows lead to stellar results, then can customize tools built to get stellar results on wide range of things those tools/workflows applied towards." Can easily apply henhouse to writing Arduino code for microcontroller project - take advantage of sweet HTTP self-hosted interface, not limited to CLI.

### Differentiator 3: Hierarchy-First Design

**Artist Control Philosophy**: Wants system where he, as artist, has full control over everything anyone else might attach to his work. Absolutely hates platforms like ETSY or Thingiverse where he gets no control over ability of others to spam his stuff with comments.

**Explicit Authority**: Much prefers explicit authority granted to trusted individuals to contribute, rather than open to public system. Design meant to sandbox collaborative efforts in ways that protect interests of folks putting in initial work to create content - in his paradigm, that means artisans.

**Cache Hierarchy Awareness**: Cache needs to be aware: if something changes, then thing containing it as child (its parent) or anything else that has "registered itself" as having links to that thing... those things ought to get marked stale/updated/recalculated.

**Flat Tables Opinion**: "Nothing wrong with flat tables and foreign keys. Loves that stuff, but wants to keep that all contained to 'main database' not cache layer. Cache layer needs to be document database."

**Class-Based Pages**: Mechanism to eliminate boilerplate so extensions (derived classes) only need to define unique behavior. Has couple great examples of power: abstract classes **work-page** and **ocr-page**.

**Permissions via Hierarchy**: Core architecture for permissions depends on hierarchy. Can mix in non-hierarchical stuff if needed - user could create derived page class that acts as interface into extra layer of non-hierarchical content in custom db schema.

### Differentiator 4: MCP Native Architecture

**What "Native" Means**: Designing every fetch API call to use same RPC2.0 envelope that MCP uses. Can configure any business logic to work for agents directly OR for humans via HTTP backend. Currently adding API backend layer to separate whitelisting, but not protocol - same data pathway.

**First-Class Citizens**: "Agents are first-class citizens because he put just as much care into their ability to access tools as he put into his own ability to access them as human user." Both MCP to run directly AND CLI to run for development work.

**Magic Glue**: "MCP provides 'magic glue' that connects chat window to real life work getting done in henhouse system. Anything he extends henhouse to do or be control point of, agents can do that for him through MCP. Without MCP would manually click and type thousand little things instead of typing one paragraph of instructions."

**Multi-Backend Forcing**: "If not for plan to have MCP right from getgo, probably would have had HTTP backend stuff hard coded in lot of places (or everywhere all at once) like legacy code. Planning for MCP integration from beginning really forced thinking about whole gateway design from multi-backend approach."

**Day-to-Day Reality**: Can play high-level architect role in planning, let agent workers keep details updated, mostly be consumer of planning website content they're creating. Examples: accounting database, puzzle project - setting up work dockets of chores for adding new folder of photos/scans to system.

**Agents Do Heavy Lifting**: Fetch calls seen as "backup plan" or "manual override." Designed system where agents do heavy lifting of content maintenance work, leaving humans to be high-level "vibe curators" who only step in for manual browser edits as necessary.

**Whitelist by Tier**: User tiers with different access levels. Whitelist different sets of tools for agents to use OR for human users to have access to, depending on authentication tier. Separation is whitelisting, not protocol.

### Differentiator 5: ext/ Folder Philosophy

**Problem Solved**: Ability to upgrade henhouse framework (hh/ folder) separate from rest of system. Allows deploying actual sites (puzzles, accounting) without worrying about locking into old version of framework core. Can carry improvements forward across all projects in lockstep.

**Guiding Principle Since 2010**: "Huge guiding principle ever since first PHP CMS system in 2010: be able to carry improvements forward across the board on all projects in lockstep. Framework separation enables this."

**Auto-Discovery**: Simply add new module folder top-level next to hh/ and it gets auto-discovered unless blacklisted. ext/ folder mainly there to help direct what gets included in scanning, and also in deployment process.

**Module Examples**: Self-doc module, accounting module can be selectively added or excluded.

**Upgrade Workflow**: During upgrades the ext folder gets preserved. Special "upgrade preserve" list inside ext folder lets ext fine-tune exactly which files need to be preserved when system gets upgraded.

**Integration Mechanism**: Auto-discovery systems through decorator scanning. Most of ext/ whitelists and blacklists have to do with deployment.

**vs Plugins/Modules**: Has no idea how plugins/modules work in other systems, or why those would be any different than what he's done here. In newer version, ext folder is just config location for other modules in their own folders.

## Section 6: Technical Journey Summary

### Timeline: 2001-2010 (PHP Era)

**2001**: Started making simple webpages (hand-coded HTML in Notepad/Dreamweaver), cookie-cutter boilerplate templates for puzzle design examples. Friend told him about PHP and MySQL - very appealing with CS background, so self-taught. Pre-Stack Overflow era: had to read specification documents online, learn databases, Apache, PHP, JavaScript from scratch.

**Original Problem**: Document growing catalog in way where system improvements benefit all pages at once in unison. Goal: get data in place once, then fidget with presentation to hearts content down the line. Early inkling of DRY principle before ever knowing about it.

**Pacific Puzzleworks Specific**: YES specifically for Pacific Puzzleworks as way to organize photos and fine-grain info tracking: designs, designers, production batches, types of woods, customer tracking info - one-system-to-rule-them-all. In beginning NOT general purpose at all.

**Initial Architecture**: Bunch of dedicated class files that all had lot of same boilerplate code. Eventually came to recognize how much boilerplate there was. THAT'S when he came up with idea to generalize it with Page base class and derived versions that inherited core CRUD features.

**Circa 2010 - Foundation Inventions**: Put in lot of work coming up with foundation that determines a lot of how Henhouse works now. Inventions from this timeframe: **5 static methods** in every page class (allow_duplicate_names, allow_null_names, auto_link_names, allow_inside_of, allow_inside) PLUS **custom wiki-like syntax**. These were ALL inventions at that timeframe.

**The Pattern**: "Can think back on MANY periods in past where went all-in programming for months on end, basically only stopping when ran out of money and had to polish it up enough to send out newsletter and try to justify to client base why he thought it was so important to refine website system AGAIN. This was the pattern."

**2010 Generalization Impetus**: Wanted to develop accounting system to track images of scanned documents, figured core he was using for puzzle info + photo management would be good crossover. Already manually managing like 10 different classes of puzzle things, didn't want to add another 10 accounting things all needing manual algorithms synchronized. 20 versions of same thing = good enough reason to refactor into general-purpose Page system.

### Timeline: 2022-2025 (Agent Era)
